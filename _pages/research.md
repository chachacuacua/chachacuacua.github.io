My research centers on a simple question: **how can developmental assessment become more useful for deciding what support a person may need next?**

Rather than treating identification as the endpoint, I am interested in the full path from understanding individual differences to designing support that is better matched, more engaging, and more accessible.

## Early social development

I study early social attention, social communication, and parent–child interaction in infancy and early childhood. My current work includes longitudinal and experimental research with children from approximately 5 months to 5 years of age, using behavioral measures and Tobii Pro eye-tracking.

## Autism and individual differences

I am interested in autism research that connects early identification with a richer characterization of individual needs. This includes questions about social attention, perceptual processing, developmental assessment, and how different profiles may inform individualized and family-centered support.

## Family processes

Family relationships shape how developmental support is delivered and sustained. I am interested in parent–child relationships, coparenting, transition to parenthood, and the ways family systems interact with developmental assessment and intervention.

## Technology-mediated assessment and intervention

I am exploring how interactive technologies can help connect assessment and intervention. Current interests include serious games, child–AI interaction, child–robot interaction, digital health, and telehealth. I am especially interested in tools that can move beyond proof-of-concept demonstrations toward affordable and scalable use in families, schools, clinics, and communities.

## Methods

My current methodological experience includes:

- Tobii Pro eye-tracking data collection and analysis
- EEG and fNIRS data collection
- Observational coding of parent–child interaction
- Developmental assessment in preschool settings
- R / RStudio and SPSS
- Linear mixed-effects modeling
- Godot Engine 4 for interactive research prototypes
